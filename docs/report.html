AI Foundations final project report

Second Look

An AI second opinion on an investment portfolio that shows what you really own, what it costs, how it would have held up in past crashes and whether you are on track for retirement, with every number computed in Python and only explained by AI.

2. Motivation

Who has the problem. Ordinary investors with a 401(k), an IRA and maybe a brokerage account, who own several funds and a few popular stocks but have never looked inside them. They are not professionals, and many do not pay for an advisor.

How they handle it today. Mostly they don't. Brokerage statements list holdings one account at a time and do not show that three funds own the same companies. Free tools exist but each answers one question (fund overlap, fees or retirement), usually after manual data entry, and general chatbots will happily do arithmetic they can get wrong.

What it costs them. Our sample personas (made up, but built from real tickers and real closing prices) show the kind of thing people miss:

  • Hidden concentration: in the mid-career sample, Apple is 13.3% of the portfolio ($24,068) once fund holdings are counted, while only 7.6% is held directly. Technology is 56.6% of the whole portfolio. (Source: Facts JSON for the mid-career sample)
  • Fees: the retiree sample pays a weighted 0.196% a year; over 30 years at the assumed 6% return, fees cost about $204,147, of which $151,094 is more than index funds would charge. One active fund (GFACX, 1.35%) costs $1,095 a year. (Source: Facts JSON for the retiree sample)
  • Fear: a repeat of the 2008-09 financial crisis would have cut the mid-career portfolio by 54.2% ($98,155) at the worst point. People who sold near the bottom locked that in.
  • How many people this affects: TODO: public data on how many households hold several funds without an advisor, with source.

Why anyone should care. These are expensive, slow-to-notice mistakes made by people who are trying to do the right thing. A tool that explains them plainly, never sells anything and never tells anyone what to trade could help without adding a new risk.

3. What is new here

None of the individual analyses is new. Look-through tools, fee calculators, crash backtests and retirement planners all exist. What is different is the combination and the guardrails:

Said honestly: professionals and paid tools go deeper than this (full holdings instead of top 10, tax-lot detail, live account links). Second Look's contribution is a free, explainable, guard-railed second opinion: the AI is used only to explain, a verifier checks every number it writes, and a safe template replaces anything that fails. Whether that is enough for real users has not been measured: no user study was run, and running one is the first next step (Section 8).

4. The agents

Python does all the finance. The agents never calculate and have no tools to call: they receive the computed Facts JSON and return structured JSON that Python validates. The orchestrator runs analyst → explainer → verifier; on a failure it allows one rewrite with the verifier's reasons, and after a second failure it shows a safe template built straight from the facts. An analysis makes at most 4 AI calls, all through llm.py, which checks the budget before each one.

AgentJobToolsModel and cap
intakeTurns messy pasted text into holdings (uploads skip AI entirely)None; text arrives inside <user_data> tags. Python keeps only rows whose ticker and numbers appear in the textMODEL_FAST, 1200 tokens

explainerWrites the cards for the chosen fluency level using only numbers in the facts, plus 2-3 questions for a fiduciary advisorNone (data only)MODEL_SMART, 1400 tokens
verifierPython checks every number, advice wording and plan ranges first; then a model reviewNone; its Python half uses the advice guard and number matcherMODEL_FAST, 500 tokens
pauseExplains a proposed trade neutrally: before/after and market history next to the user's time horizonNone (data only)MODEL_SMART, 700 tokens

Which models and why. Both MODEL_FAST and MODEL_SMART are gpt-6-luna through Portkey, the course default at $0.10 / $0.50 per million input / output tokens, with reasoning_effort=low. The larger models are priced in the code (gpt-6-sol $2 / $10, gpt-6-astra $10 / $50) but were not needed: on luna, 89% of first drafts passed the verifier in the quality run (Section 5), and astra's worst case per call exceeds the $0.05 per-call limit.

What was done to cut cost

  • All arithmetic in Python; the AI sees a compact Facts JSON (kept under 2,000 tokens) instead of raw data.
  • The cheapest course model (gpt-6-luna) for every agent; larger models priced but unused.
  • reasoning_effort=low: at the default effort, hidden reasoning sometimes used the whole token allowance and returned nothing, forcing retries and fallbacks.
  • A response cache, a hard cap of 4 AI calls per analysis, and budget limits per call ($0.05), per session ($0.10) and per day ($2.00), all enforced in budget.py before each call.
  • Demo mode replays recorded answers for $0.

6. Safety

Safety policy. Second Look explains numbers; it never gives advice. It never tells anyone to buy, sell or hold a specific security, never says which account to move money into or out of, never sets price targets and never promises returns. Every number shown is computed in Python; the AI may only explain numbers it is given, and a verifier rejects any draft with a number that is not in the facts, advice-like wording, or a retirement figure that is not a labeled range. Uploaded files are processed in memory, account numbers and free text are removed before anything reaches the AI, and the API key lives only in .env, which git never tracks. When anything fails, users see a safe summary built straight from the numbers.

Threats and defenses

ThreatDefense
Prompt injection in pasted text or CSV fieldsPause Button text is parsed by Python and never sent to the AI; pasted text goes inside <user_data> tags with closing tags neutralised; CSV descriptions and notes are dropped; intake rows are kept only if their ticker and numbers appear in the text
Data leaks (key, account numbers, system prompts)Only config.py reads settings; logs and errors are redacted; account numbers become 'Account 1'; a secret scanner checks every tracked file and the rendered app (tests/test_secrets.py); gitleaks runs on every commit
Invented factsThe verifier matches every number to the Facts JSON within the writer's rounding; the analyst's ranking uses Python-looked-up dollars, not numbers the model wrote
Wrong actionsAgents have no tools and cannot trade or send anything; advice_guard flags buy/sell/hold, account moves, price targets and promises
LoopsAt most 4 AI calls per analysis and one retry per call; then a safe template
Overspendingbudget.py checks per-call ($0.05), per-session ($0.10) and daily ($2.00) limits before every call; scripts add --max-usd; every call is recorded in the ledger and shown on the Admin page

8. Limits and next steps

What does not work yet

  • Fund look-through uses only each fund's top ~10 holdings, so company exposures are minimums.
  • Prices come from Yahoo Finance (unofficial) with an offline snapshot through 2026-10-16; sample portfolios always use the snapshot.
  • The retirement plan is simple: constant real returns, a flat 15% tax on pre-tax accounts and no other taxes.
  • The glide-path reference and the risk-profile ranges are placeholders until a cited source is added.
  • The stricter plan-range rule makes the model verifier send more first drafts back for a rewrite. Its reasons are now recorded on the Admin page, but the rule has not been tuned yet.
  • Demo mode has saved answers only for the three samples with their default answers; uploads get the standard summary.
  • No user study has been run; the app is not deployed online; the macOS setup is untested.

With another month

  • Run a user study (5-6 people, timed against a spreadsheet, with a comprehension check).
  • Full fund holdings from fund filings instead of top 10.
  • Tune the verifier using its recorded reasons to cut rewrites; run the MODEL_SMART quality comparison.
  • Deploy online with proper secret handling.
  • Monte Carlo ranges instead of three fixed scenarios; basic tax awareness.
